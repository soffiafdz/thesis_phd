# QC Training Video Transcripts

Summaries of the QC training videos used in the Qrater study
(Chapter 3). The original training videos cannot be shared due to privacy
considerations for the trainees and the clinical data shown. These transcripts
were produced using [summarize.tech](https://www.summarize.tech).

## Technical Summary of the Training Video for Raw MRI

In this video, the speaker examines different MRI cases and evaluates their image quality. They observe various artifacts, signal drop-off, and non-uniformities, but overall, most of the cases shown pass the quality control (QC) criteria. The speaker leaves comments for the anomalies observed, suggesting further investigation during the processing QC stage. The speaker then focuses on the eligibility of different data sets for passing MRI acquisition quality control. They highlight various aspects that are commonly considered, including features like high intensity voxels that limit dynamic range, slight ringing artifacts, and the presence of fat in specific anatomical regions. The speaker explains how these factors can influence the quality of the images and whether they would meet the requirements for quality control.

(00:00:00 - 00:05:00). The speaker states that the goal is to demonstrate how to perform quality control (QC) on raw MRI data using the Qrater software. The software displays JPEG images of specific subjects, with each subject having one JPEG image per imaging modality. The images show different planes---axial, sagittal, and coronal---providing a comprehensive view of the brain anatomy. During QC, several checks are performed, including assessing the intensities to ensure the image is neither too dark nor too bright. The coverage of the brain from left to right, front to back, and top to bottom is also evaluated, along with the absence of artifacts caused by movement, flow, or wrap around. Multiple examples are shown to illustrate both passing and thresholded passing data sets, highlighting the importance of assessing image quality before further processing.

(00:05:00 - 00:10:00). The speaker highlights the importance of intensity normalization in the image processing pipeline to correct for brightness discrepancies. They examine images with good coverage and dynamic intensity range, but observe dark areas in the white matter indicating vascular lesions. Despite this, the overall quality of the images is deemed satisfactory. The speaker also mentions bright artifacts in the scalp due to fat content and explains the wraparound artifact caused by a small field of view. Lastly, they examine a case with a bright artifact in the front of the brain and note that while the coverage is good, the intensities appear dark for the rest of the brain. (00:10:00 - 00:15:00). The speaker points out that certain artifacts, such as brightness at the front and darkness at the back of an image, can be corrected by the imaging processing pipeline, allowing the data set to pass QC. They explain that dynamic range reduction due to these artifacts makes it challenging for intensity normalization and non-uniform correction steps to correct them. The speaker emphasizes the importance of examining the processed data to determine if the intensity contrast is acceptable before making a final decision. ## Technical Summary of the Training Video for Linear Registration

In the video titled "QC linear stx reg2", the speaker discusses quality control of linear stereotactic registration. They demonstrate the use of the Qrater program to analyze different databases of images and evaluate the quality of stereotactic registration. The speaker examines various data sets, assessing the fit and symmetry of brain contours. They highlight cases where the registration passes or fails, identifying specific errors such as incorrect orientation or scaling. The speaker also discusses issues with z-scaling, rotation, and overall size of the data set. They emphasize the importance of checking scaling, contours, and symmetry to ensure the quality of the MRI scans.

(00:00:00 - 00:05:00). The speaker discusses quality control of linear stereotactic registration. They explain that stereotactic space is defined by specific points, but manual identification of these points is difficult, so automatic techniques have been developed. To ensure proper transformation and registration of subject data to stereotactic space, quality control is necessary. The speaker introduces the Qrater program, which allows them to analyze different databases of images. They demonstrate how to evaluate the quality of stereotactic registration by visually assessing the fit of the contour of the unbiased stereotactic template to the MRI image. They emphasize the importance of checking the overall orientation and size of the data set, as well as ensuring that the cortex fits well with the data. The speaker showcases several slices of data and points out areas where the registration seems to align well and areas where there are slight differences.

(00:05:00 - 00:10:00). The speaker examines several data sets to assess the fit and symmetry of brain contours. The first example shows a well-fitting and symmetrical data set, with only a slight extension in the back indicating some residual variability. Another data set with atrophy also fits well, except for some areas where cerebrospinal fluid is present. Overall, these are considered Pass. However, the speaker also demonstrates cases where the QC fails, such as when the brain contours exceed the MRI image vertically or when the entire temporal lobe is outside the contour. These failures are attributed to issues with z-scaling. The speaker demonstrates how to add comments and mark these cases as failures.

(00:10:00 - 00:15:00). The speaker reviews different images and evaluates their registration success. They highlight cases where the registration passes, fails catastrophically, or has specific errors such as incorrect orientation or scaling. The speaker examines the symmetry and contour fit of various brain regions and identifies issues like misregistration and scaling errors. They also point out cases where the MRI does not align correctly with the contour. Overall, they provide a detailed analysis of the registration quality for each image.

(00:15:00 - 00:20:00). The speaker discusses various issues and failures in the quality control of MRI scans. They point out instances where the z scale is too small, causing the data to fail. They also highlight cases where the overall size of the data set is too small in various dimensions. In some cases, the brain appears too large and extends past the contours. There are also instances where the intensity of the image is not aligned with the MRI contours. The speaker identifies problems with rotation, scale, and fit, ultimately leading to failures in the quality control process.

(00:20:00 - 00:25:00). The speaker discusses the issue of rotation in MRI images. They point out that the rotations in the images seem to be okay, with the orientation of the brain looking good. However, there are some concerns about the scaling and rotation, as the tectorium and cerebellum do not fit well in the sagittal images. The speaker also mentions a rotation error related to the orbital frontal contour cutting through the occipital fat. They emphasize the importance of checking the scaling, contours at the top of the brain, base of the cerebellum, and temporal lobes. Additionally, they highlight the need for left-right symmetry in both transverse and coronal images. Overall, these issues should be addressed to ensure the quality of the MRI.

## Technical Summary of the Training Video of the Follow-Up Meeting: Linear Registration of ADNI Data

In this video, the linear stx QC is examined through various examples of brain images. The reviewer evaluates each case based on scale, intensity, contour fitting, and symmetry. While there is some disagreement among the reviewers regarding the pass or fail status of certain cases, they discuss the limitations of linear registration in handling protrusions or asymmetries in the brain. They emphasize the importance of evaluating the accuracy of linear registration rather than purely relying on the image's appearance. The reviewers also discuss the impact of Z scale on assessing asymmetry, as well as the presence of shear or asymmetry in the Z axis. They analyze different data sets and debate whether certain cases should pass or fail, considering factors such as contour fitting and signal drop-off. The reviewer highlights the need for proper alignment using linear registration and provides detailed analysis and recommendations for improvement based on the intra-rater experiment results. The reviewer also mentions the feedback received from participants regarding the scale and shape of the brain, acknowledging the challenges in accurately judging images with slight deviations from the standard model.
